\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Dataset and Protocol}

MFNet contains 784 aligned RGB-thermal training pairs and 392 test pairs captured in
urban driving scenes during day and night \cite{ha2017mfnet}. We use the eight
foreground classes plus the unlabeled class, report the official nine-class mIoU used
by the training code, and additionally report foreground mIoU and nighttime pedestrian
IoU. The nighttime subset is defined by the ``N'' prefix in the MFNet filenames, the
same convention used by the benchmark. All models use the CMNeXt-B2 backbone with a
SegFormer-B2 initialization \cite{xie2021segformer,zhang2023delivering}, a batch size of
eight, AdamW with a learning rate of $6\times10^{-5}$, and 120 epochs. The RGB-only and
thermal-only experts are trained with the same schedule and augmentation; the only
difference is the input modality and, for the thermal expert, the resolution of the
thermal stream.

Low-resolution thermal is simulated by a bicubic round trip, as described in
Section~3.1. We evaluate three budgets: $60\times80$ (\lr{8}, $1/64$ of the RGB
pixels), $120\times160$ (\lr{4}, $1/16$), and $240\times320$ (\lr{2}, $1/4$). The
full-resolution RGB-T teacher is a two-stream CMNeXt-B2 trained on both full-resolution
streams;
we use it only as a reference for the recovery calculation and do not place it in the
main table, because its schedule is longer than the 120-epoch experts.

\subsection{How Many Thermal Pixels Are Needed?}

Table~\ref{tab:budget} and Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} summarize the budget sweep. The
thermal-only model improves monotonically on nighttime pedestrians as the budget grows:
57.21 IoU at $60\times80$, 62.41 at $120\times160$, and 64.73 at $240\times320$. The
corresponding recovery of the full-resolution RGB-T teacher's nighttime advantage is
58\%, 84\%, and 95\%. A full-resolution thermal-only B2 trained for this comparison
reaches 65.61 nighttime pedestrian IoU, so the denominator is not inflated by RGB
access. The knee is therefore at $120\times160$: doubling the linear
resolution from $60\times80$ buys 5.2 points, while doubling it again buys only 2.3.
At the same time, overall thermal-only mIoU remains far below RGB
(32.31, 41.67, 42.80 versus 56.38), because the thermal stream loses on cars, curbs,
guardrails, and road markings at every budget. The budget curve is a statement about
the safety class, not about overall parsing.

\subsection{The Gate Makes the Budget Usable}

Table~\ref{tab:budget} also reports the gate. At $120\times160$ the constrained gate
reaches $57.48\pm0.11$ official mIoU and $62.26\pm1.02$ nighttime pedestrian IoU,
against 56.38 and 45.24 for RGB-only and 41.67 and 62.41 for the thermal expert. The
gate therefore improves overall mIoU by 1.10 and nighttime pedestrian IoU by 17.0 over
RGB, while giving up only 0.15 nighttime pedestrian IoU relative to the thermal expert.
The latter trade is deliberate: the thermal expert is better on pedestrians but much
worse on the other classes, and the gate recovers overall parsing without surrendering
the safety gain. At $60\times80$ the gate is $57.19\pm0.07$ and $58.63\pm0.23$: the
cheapest stream already matches RGB overall, but concedes 3.7 nighttime pedestrian
points to $120\times160$. At $240\times320$ it reaches $57.54\pm0.06$ and
$63.29\pm0.64$, only 0.06 overall and 1.03 pedestrian points above $120\times160$ for
four times the pixels. The $120\times160$ operating point is therefore the efficiency
knee; $240\times320$ is a marginal accuracy purchase, not a different regime.

Table~\ref{tab:variants} isolates the deployment variants at $120\times160$ (seed
3407). The full gate, which may override RGB wherever the experts disagree, reaches
57.58 overall and 61.42 nighttime pedestrian IoU. The person-constrained gate reaches
57.39 and 61.42, a cost of 0.19 overall at the same pedestrian score. Restricting
overrides to the thermal expert's pedestrian prediction (\texttt{person\_b}) or to a
pedestrian probability threshold (\texttt{person\_prob}) is worse, 57.23 overall,
because the RGB expert sometimes predicts the pedestrian correctly and should not be
overridden. The person-or rule is the only variant that preserves both experts'
pedestrian evidence.

\subsection{Where the Gain Comes From}

Figure~\ref{fig:perclass} shows the per-class change from RGB to the person-or gate.
The entire gain is the pedestrian class: $+8.91$ IoU, from 55.79 to 64.70. All other
classes change by at most 0.08 IoU, and seven of the eight change by at most 0.06. This
is the intended behavior of a structural safety prior: it repairs the class that
motivates the thermal sensor and leaves the rest of the scene to the higher-resolution
RGB expert. The unconstrained gate reaches a higher overall mIoU, but it pays for that
with car-stop $-1.96$ and guardrail $+1.60$: it is a different operating point, not a
free improvement.

\subsection{Robustness to Corrupted RGB}

We corrupt RGB at test time with the same hard-condition family used for gate training:
a contrast reduction to 0.55 and additive Gaussian noise. The thermal
stream is left at its budget. Table~\ref{tab:degraded} reports the result for seed 3407. RGB-only collapses from 56.38 to 47.79
official mIoU and from 45.24 to 33.78 nighttime pedestrian IoU. The thermal expert is
unaffected by the RGB corruption and retains 41.67 and 62.41. The full gate reaches
51.27 overall and 61.59 nighttime pedestrian IoU, the best overall result under
corruption. The person-or gate reaches 49.34 and 61.59: its fixed constraint prevents
it from using thermal evidence on non-person classes that RGB can no longer parse,
which is the expected cost of the safety prior. The clean/degraded trade-off is the
honest limitation of a fixed constraint; an image-quality-conditioned gate is future
work.

\subsection{Ablations and Cost}

Hard-condition augmentation is neutral for the person-constrained gate. With it, the
person-or gate reaches 57.39 clean and 49.34 corrupted; without it, 57.47 and 49.27.
The difference is within the seed spread. The augmentation matters only for the
unconstrained gate under corruption: it reaches 51.27 with augmentation and 49.31
without, at 57.58 versus 57.72 clean. This is the expected pattern. Hard examples teach
the gate where thermal can replace RGB on non-person classes, but the person constraint
forbids those overrides, so the constrained gate cannot convert the extra training
signal into a robustness gain. The constraint, not the augmentation, is what fixes the
non-person invariance.

Table~\ref{tab:compute} places the gate against two single-model alternatives. A
six-channel early-fusion CMNeXt-B2 concatenates RGB and thermal at the input and matches the RGB compute
(25.8M parameters, 46.4 GFLOPs, 11.1 ms), but reaches only
55.51 overall and 60.57 nighttime pedestrian IoU: it recovers some thermal evidence but
loses on the non-person classes and remains below RGB. A single two-stream full-fusion
CMNeXt-B2 reaches 55.80 and 61.88, but costs 58.7M parameters, 74.3 GFLOPs, and 29.8 ms.
The gate has 53.5M parameters, 138.0 GFLOPs, and 24.0 ms: 2.2 times the RGB-only
latency, but 19\% \emph{faster} than the full-fusion model, with $+1.68$ overall and
$+0.38$ nighttime pedestrian IoU over it, and $+1.97$/$+1.69$ over the compute-matched
early-fusion model. The reason is that both experts are 25.8M single-modality models;
the usual two-model overhead comes from pairing RGB with a 58.7M full-fusion expert. The
remaining 2.2 times RGB is the price of the second forward pass. A native low-resolution
thermal branch, or distilling the gate into the RGB expert, would remove it; we leave
both to future work.

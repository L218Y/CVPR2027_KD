\section{Method}

\subsection{Two Frozen Experts and a Budgeted Thermal Stream}

Let $x\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times H\times W}$ be the RGB image and $t\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times
H\times W}$ the thermal image, both at the RGB resolution. The thermal stream is
budgeted by a scale factor $s\in\{2,4,8\}$: we downsample $t$ to $(H/s)\times(W/s)$
with bicubic interpolation and upsample it back to $(H,W)$ before it reaches the
network. This round trip is a conservative proxy for a native low-resolution sensor:
it removes high-frequency thermal detail without inventing it, and it keeps the
segmentation loader, augmentation, and evaluation identical across budgets. At
$s=4$ the sensor has $120\times160$ pixels, one sixteenth of the RGB pixel count.

Two experts are trained independently and then frozen. The RGB expert $f_a$ maps $x$
to a probability map $p_a=f_a(x)\in\Delta^{K}$; the thermal expert $f_b$ maps the
budgeted thermal image to $p_b=f_b(t)\in\Delta^{K}$. Both use the same CMNeXt-B2
backbone and the same 120-epoch schedule, so the only difference between them is the
modality and, for $f_b$, the thermal budget. The experts are deliberately not fused
during training: a globally fused model would learn to rely on thermal for every class,
which is exactly the failure we observe at low resolution.

To separate the effect of routing from the effect of fusion, we also train a
six-channel early-fusion CMNeXt-B2. It concatenates the RGB and budgeted thermal images
at the input, expands the first patch-embedding convolution from three to six channels,
and otherwise uses the same backbone and schedule. The expansion is initialized from the
RGB pre-trained filters, so the model has essentially the RGB parameter count and FLOPs. It
is a compute-matched single-model alternative, not a second copy of the gate.

\subsection{Person-Aware Reliability Gate}

The gate is a small U-Net $G_\theta$ with four downsampling stages and a
single-channel output. Its input is the concatenation
\begin{equation}
  z = \bigl[\,x,\; t,\; p_a,\; p_b,\; \max_k p_a,\; \max_k p_b,\; H(p_a),\; H(p_b)\,\bigr],
  \label{eq:features}
\end{equation}
where $H(p)=-\sum_k p_k\log p_k$ is the per-pixel entropy. The probability maps expose
the experts' hypotheses, the maxima expose their confidence, and the entropies expose
their uncertainty; the raw images let the gate detect conditions in which one modality
is structurally more reliable. The gate outputs a logit $g=G_\theta(z)$ and the
deployment rule is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat{y}_i &= D_i\,\hat{y}_{b,i} + (1-D_i)\,\hat{y}_{a,i},\\
D_i &= \mathbb{1}[g_i>0]\,\mathbb{1}[\hat{y}_{a,i}\ne\hat{y}_{b,i}]\\
&\quad\times \mathbb{1}[\hat{y}_{a,i}=c_p \lor \hat{y}_{b,i}=c_p].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:deploy}
\end{equation}
where $c_p$ is the pedestrian class and $\hat y_{a,i}=\arg\max_k p_{a,i,k}$, $\hat y_{b,i}=\arg\max_k p_{b,i,k}$ are the experts' predicted labels. The first two conditions restrict overrides to
pixels where the experts disagree and the gate is confident; the third is the
structural person constraint. It encodes the safety requirement that thermal may
repair pedestrians but may not overwrite RGB's higher-resolution evidence on cars,
curbs, guardrails, road markings, or the other seven classes.

The gate is trained on the training split only. For each batch we sample a
hard-condition augmentation with probability $p_{\text{hard}}=0.5$: RGB is darkened by
a random factor in $[0.35,1]$, blurred with a $5\times5$ average filter with
probability $0.5$, and corrupted by Gaussian noise; the thermal stream receives a small
Gaussian perturbation. The gate is supervised only on disagreement pixels, with a
binary target that is one when the thermal expert is correct and zero when the RGB
expert is correct:
\begin{equation}
\resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
  \mathcal{L}(\theta) =
  \frac{\sum_i w_i\,v_i\,
  \mathrm{BCE}\bigl(g_i,\;\mathbb{1}[y_{b,i}=y_i]\bigr)}
  {\sum_i w_i\,v_i},
  \qquad
  v_i=\mathbb{1}[y_{a,i}\ne y_{b,i}],\quad
  w_i=1+3\,\mathbb{1}[y_i=c_p].$}
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
Pixels where both experts agree, or where the label is ignored, do not contribute. The
person weight makes the gate pay attention to the class that motivates the sensor, but
it does not change the deployment constraint. We use ten epochs, a batch size of two,
and Adam with a learning rate of $10^{-3}$; the gate has 1.9M parameters.

\subsection{Selection Protocol}

The official MFNet test split is used once per model. To select the gate and its
hyperparameters without touching that split, we partition the 784 training images into
627 fit images and 157 gate-validation images, stratified by day/night condition. The gate is trained on the 627 fit images with hard-condition
augmentation and selected on the 157 gate-validation images under the same hard
augmentation. The selection score is the gate-validation mIoU plus half the
gate-validation pedestrian IoU, which balances overall parsing and the safety class.
The official test split is evaluated only after the checkpoint is frozen. The same
protocol is used for all three gate seeds and for the low-resolution sweep, so the
numbers in Section~\ref{sec:experiments} are not selected on test.

The protocol matters because the training and test distributions are not equally
difficult. On the clean gate-validation split the deployed gate reaches 79.88 mIoU,
whereas the same model reaches 57.48 on the official test. A per-class selector chosen
on clean training accuracy therefore sees a much easier problem than deployment and
collapses to the thermal expert on every class, losing overall mIoU. Selecting on a
held-out, hard-augmented split is the minimal correction; it does not eliminate the
domain gap, but it prevents the selector from being fooled by clean training data.

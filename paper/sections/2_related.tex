\section{Related Work}

\subsection{RGB-Thermal Semantic Segmentation}

RGB-T segmentation fuses a visible image with a thermal image so that the thermal
stream can supply evidence when visible illumination fails. MFNet introduced the first
aligned day/night benchmark and a two-stream encoder for real-time driving scenes
\cite{ha2017mfnet}; RTFNet scaled the idea with deeper encoders and a lightweight
decoder \cite{sun2019rtfnet}. Later work replaced hand-designed fusion with
cross-modal attention and transformer blocks. CMX exchanges features between an RGB
backbone and an auxiliary modality through a Cross-Modal Feature Rectification Module
and a Feature Fusion Module \cite{zhang2022crossmodal}, while CMNeXt and the DeLiVER
benchmark extend the recipe to an arbitrary set of modalities and to missing-modality
inference \cite{zhang2023delivering}. Recent variants add spectral-aware global
fusion \cite{zhang2025spectralaware}, state-space models for real-time wild-scene
segmentation \cite{guo2025crossmodal}, modality-aware pre-training and rectification
\cite{guo2025tuni}, language guidance \cite{zhao2025unveiling}, frequency-guided
fusion \cite{cantez2026frequencyguided}, and broader RGB-X fusion
\cite{cao2026multimodal,zhang2026crossweaver,reddy2026seeing}. Across this line of
work, the thermal stream is assumed to be full resolution and always available; the
sensor-cost question is not part of the architecture search.

\subsection{Thermal Benchmarks and Low-Light Perception}

MFNet remains the reference for daytime/nighttime urban RGB-T segmentation and is the
benchmark on which most fusion architectures report \cite{ha2017mfnet}. PST900 targets
subterranean robot perception with synchronized RGB-thermal pairs and four object
classes \cite{shivakumar2019rgbthermal}, and Freiburg Thermal targets the day-night
domain gap with a large unlabeled capture and a small annotated test set
\cite{vertens2020heatnet}. Thermal-only benchmarks such as SODA provide day and night
annotations without paired RGB \cite{li2019soda}. These datasets make clear that
thermal imagery is valuable precisely when visible evidence is weak, but they do not
isolate the resolution of the thermal stream: the sensor is either full resolution or
absent. Our budget sweep fills that gap by treating thermal resolution as the
controlled variable and measuring the safety class separately from the other classes.

\subsection{Cross-Modal Transfer and Routing}

A natural alternative to carrying a thermal sensor is to transfer its knowledge into
RGB. Multimodal distillation and prototype-based transfer have been used to compress
or robustify multimodal segmentation \cite{tan2025rmmss,wu2024fusionthendistillation},
and complementary masking trains RGB-T models to survive missing modalities
\cite{shin2023complementary}. Our diagnosis differs from these efforts in target: we
ask whether the nighttime pedestrian advantage itself can be moved, and we find that it
cannot. The result is consistent with the broader observation that a student cannot
recover information that is absent from its input, no matter how the distillation loss
is shaped.

Routing between experts is a separate line of work. Learning-to-defer methods learn
when a classifier should abstain in favor of a human or another model, with
theoretical guarantees for single- and multi-expert settings
\cite{mao2023principled,montreuil2025onestage,liu2026when}. Mixture-of-experts models
learn input-dependent gates over specialized predictors and have been analyzed for
softmax gating and expert underfitting \cite{bariletto2026bayesian,nguyen2025convergence}.
Our gate borrows the deferral view but operates per pixel and between two dense
segmentation experts, and it is trained only on disagreement pixels with a
safety-class weight. The person constraint is a structural prior, not a learned
classifier, which keeps the thermal expert from touching the eight classes on which
RGB is stronger.

\subsection{Calibration and Reliability}

A gate that reads confidence must reckon with miscalibration. Modern networks are
often overconfident, and temperature scaling or related post-hoc corrections are the
standard remedy \cite{guo2017calibration}. In our setting, the RGB expert is
confidently wrong at night: its mean maximum softmax probability is 0.983 on both clean
and degraded inputs, so a threshold on RGB confidence cannot separate the two regimes.
The gate therefore reads the full probability maps, their entropy, and the thermal
image, rather than a single confidence scalar. This design choice is also why the gate
degrades gracefully: when RGB is corrupted, the thermal expert's probability map
becomes the more informative input, and the gate can route accordingly.
